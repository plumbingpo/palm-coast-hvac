Clear Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Nobody likes surprises on the final bill. So Palm Coast HVAC quotes every Palm Coast job before the work begins, after a complete diagnostic that covers the equipment's age, fuel type, and tonnage, refrigerant levels, coil and heat exchanger condition, and the ductwork feeding each room. You'll know what's wrong, what the fix involves, and what it costs before you say yes.

The problem with HVAC failures is that many of them develop out of sight. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil slowly drains refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down a little at a time, and a weak capacitor holds on until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. If your bills are climbing, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Plenty of Palm Coast homeowners ask us to size up an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical AC or furnace lifespan of 12 to 18 years, a replacement on your own timeline beats scrambling for a new unit during a cold snap. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your equipment has left.

Seasonal Tune-Ups

Seasonal HVAC Maintenance Plans

Each maintenance visit from Palm Coast HVAC is far more than a quick filter swap. Our technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, replaces the filter, and measures static pressure across the ducts. Small problems are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and the equipment stays running at full efficiency.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with spring cooling tune-ups and fall heating tune-ups for most systems. That's especially important in dusty areas, where dirty condenser coils choke heat rejection and drive up energy use. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your equipment's age, your household's comfort demands, and your local climate.

The savings add up quickly. Clean coils move heat more efficiently, a correct refrigerant charge keeps the compressor from overworking, and a fresh filter protects the blower motor. Compared with the cost of replacing a system even one year early, a maintenance plan is money well spent.

Right-Sized Systems

The Right Size System for Your Home

An undersized system runs nonstop and never catches up on the hottest days, while an oversized one short-cycles, wastes energy, and leaves the house humid. Palm Coast HVAC sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual home. That means a proper load calculation that accounts for square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout, rather than simply matching the tonnage of the old unit. For ductless mini-splits, it means matching head capacity to each zone so every room holds its setpoint.

Houses change over time. A finished basement, an addition, or two new bedrooms can outgrow the single-stage system that once kept up just fine. Modern two-stage and variable-speed heat pumps hold tighter temperatures and use much less energy. If you're torn between a heat pump and a furnace-and-AC pair, or ducted and ductless, we'll lay out the real trade-offs in SEER2 ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting needs, and installation work.

Repairs We Handle

The HVAC Repairs Palm Coast Homes Need Most

The majority of service calls we take in Palm Coast come down to a handful of familiar problems. The air conditioner blows warm air. The furnace won't light, or it lights and shuts off minutes later. The evaporator coil freezes solid, the condenser hums without starting, or a mini-split starts flashing error codes. Add refrigerant leaks, clogged drains, and forgetful thermostats, and you have the full list. Our technicians handle all of them, always targeting the component that actually failed.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A weak capacitor simply keeps the compressor from starting, but a cracked heat exchanger can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a blocked condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling. We match the fix to the failure: capacitors, contactors, and fan motors on cooling systems, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards on furnaces, sealed and repaired ductwork where conditioned air is bleeding into the attic, and refrigerant recovered and recharged after a repaired leak. Whatever you're facing, our team has the training and parts to handle it.
